# ai sdr cost: what you actually pay per booked meeting, from $64/mo setters to $2,500/mo agents

The `ai sdr cost` question almost always starts in the same place: someone saw a homepage that said "$99/month" and then got a quote for $2,500. Both numbers were real. They just described different things.

An AI SDR bill is usually three bills wearing one logo. There's the AI layer (the agent that reasons and writes), the data layer (who you're allowed to contact), and the delivery layer (mailboxes, domains, warmup). Vendors bundle them in different combinations, and the bundle is where the surprises live.

This piece breaks down what these tools actually charge per meeting, where the hidden line items sit, and how a per-message platform like CloseBot compares to the per-agent pricing most AI SDRs use.

## The sticker price was never the price

List prices across the category run from roughly $600/month for entry-level automation to $5,000+/month for a fully managed AI SDR, with most agentic tools landing between $2,000 and $4,000/month. Real spend typically runs 1.5x to 2x the advertised figure once data and sending are added.

That gap exists because of what's inside the bundle:

- **The AI layer.** The agent that decides who to contact and writes the message.
- **The data layer.** Contact records, emails, enrichment, verification.
- **The delivery layer.** Mailboxes, secondary domains, warmup, inbox rotation.

Some tools sell all three. Others sell you the brain and quietly assume you already have a contact list and warmed mailboxes. That single distinction explains most of the invoice shock in this category.

The human SDR comparison is the one buyers keep in their head: roughly $84,000/year all-in with salary, benefits, tooling and management overhead. That's the number an AI SDR has to beat. It usually does on paper.

## Cost per meeting is the only number that survives scrutiny

Monthly fees are easy to compare and almost useless. A $3,000/month tool that books 12 meetings costs $250 per meeting. A $1,200/month tool that books 10 costs $120. The cheaper tool is worse value here, which is exactly backwards from how most buyers shop.

Here's what published numbers look like for a heavily marketed agentic option. AiSDR's Explore plan runs $900/month for 1,200 lead search credits and 1,200 AI messages, with the company projecting about 3 meetings a month. That works out to roughly **$300 per meeting** and **$0.75 per message**. The Grow plan at $2,500/month covers 4,500 messages with about 11 meetings projected, which is roughly **$227 per meeting** and **$0.56 per message**.

Two details matter more than the headline:

1. **Follow-ups count against the quota.** 1,200 messages is not 1,200 prospects. A five-touch sequence means you're really reaching about 240 new contacts a month on the entry tier.
2. **Billing is quarterly.** That's $2,700 upfront before the first meeting lands, and new mailboxes need 30–60 days to warm up before they can carry real volume.

Benchmarks from other providers put a healthy outbound motion somewhere between $80 and $250 per booked meeting. Below $150 is strong. Above $300, your deal size has to justify it — fine if you're closing $50K contracts, painful if your average deal is $5K.

## Where the AI SDR budget actually leaks

Run through this checklist before signing anything.

**Data.** This is the big one. Per-contact enrichment caps and overage fees dominate renewal invoices, and cheap data burns sending reputation at the same time. Emailing a list that's 30% invalid does three expensive things: you pay for bounces, you damage your domain, and you conclude "AI SDRs don't work" when the problem was the list. Contact data decays at roughly 2–3% per month, so verification before send isn't optional at volume.

**Mailboxes and warmup.** Sending 2,000 cold emails a day safely means 40–60 inboxes across multiple domains. Some platforms include this. Others leave you to spend $200–600/month on sending infrastructure separately.

**Seats and overage.** Per-agent pricing scales linearly and overage rates are usually punitive. Three AI SDRs is three times the bill.

**Onboarding and managed services.** Enterprise vendors frequently attach a $2,000–10,000 setup or "strategist" fee that never appears on the pricing page.

Allocate roughly like this if you want the math to hold: about 40% to data accuracy, 25% to sending infrastructure, 25% to the AI and sequencing layer, 10% to reporting. The AI itself being a minority of the budget surprises people, but it's the honest split.

## Where the per-message model beats per-agent pricing

The two pricing shapes fail in different places.

Per-agent pricing punishes you for growing. Every new sending seat is a new subscription, and follow-up messages eat your quota whether or not they book anything. Enterprise floors start around $3,000/month, and most vendors now gate real numbers behind a demo call.

A per-message platform does the reverse. You keep the platform fee flat and let the reply volume climb; the effective rate improves as you scale. The catch is that booking performance stops being someone else's problem. Nothing in CloseBot's published pricing promises a meetings-per-message ratio, because that depends on your offer, your channels, your follow-up timing and your qualification criteria — not on the software.

So the honest way to compare is arithmetically. If your CRM inbox gets 2,000 inbound replies a month and your agent books 5% of them, that's 100 meetings. On the $109 plan that's roughly **$1.09 per booked meeting** in platform cost, before your CRM subscription. A per-agent AI SDR at $2,500/month booking 11 meetings is at $227 per meeting. Run those two numbers against your own booking rate before assuming the famous brand is the expensive one — or the cheap one.

That arithmetic is also a warning. A per-message model rewards volume, and volume with bad qualification produces a full calendar of meetings nobody wants. Set the qualification bar before you raise the message ceiling.

## Hidden costs to add to any AI SDR quote

- **Warmup window.** New mailboxes take 30–60 days to reach full sending capacity. Your first month rarely performs, which matters a lot if you've paid a quarter upfront.
- **Verification.** Bounce checks before sending protect the domain. Cheap insurance, non-negotiable at scale.
- **Additional seats.** CloseBot charges $5 per extra user seat; agencies can rebill that too. On other platforms, a seat can mean a full second license.
- **Storage.** Business plans start at 1 MB of upload storage with add-ons priced from $0.10 to $3.00 per MB per month depending on how much you need.
- **Setup and "strategist" fees.** Common at the enterprise end, usually invisible until the contract.
- **The CRM underneath.** Easy to forget, and it's the reason a "cheap" agent sometimes isn't.

**What to do with all of this**

1. **Work backward from pipeline.** If a closed deal is worth $10,000 and you close one in five meetings, you can afford up to roughly $400 per meeting and still come out ahead. Most healthy motions target $80–250.
2. **Size volume from leads, not from features.** Your data and sending needs are driven by how many qualified contacts you need per month, not by the tool's feature list.
3. **Force every vendor to itemize.** Ask what you'd pay for the AI, the data and the sending separately, even if you're buying a bundle. It's the single best negotiation lever you have.
4. **Add a 1.5x buffer.** Overage, verification, extra mailboxes and onboarding reliably push real spend above the quote.
5. **Pick the pricing shape that matches your channel.** Cold email outreach fits per-agent tools with their own data.
